# 1. Canada's economy, explained simply, and how it differs from the U.S., India and the rest of the world

*Prepared October 2026. Figures marked (verify) come from secondary sources or memory and must be re-checked before external use. See [sources.md](sources.md).*

## The 60-second version

Canada is a wealthy, mid-sized country (about 41 million people, roughly an eighth of the U.S.). It is **not "a smaller, colder U.S."**. Three facts shape almost every business opportunity there:

1. **It is ten economies stitched together.** Provinces control employment law, sales tax, privacy, professional licensing, liquor, securities and much of health and education. Something legal in Ontario can break the law in Quebec.
2. **It lives on trade with one customer.** About three-quarters of Canada's goods exports have historically gone to the United States (verify current share). When U.S. trade policy changes, Canadian businesses feel it within weeks.
3. **A few big players run key industries.** Five or six banks, three telecom carriers, two airlines, provincial liquor monopolies and quota-managed dairy and poultry. Prices are higher and change is slower, and the gaps sit in the spaces these incumbents ignore: small businesses and edge cases.

On top of that, 2026 is a stress year: the U.S. declined to renew CUSMA at the July 1 review and added new tariffs that override it. The mortgage-renewal wave also peaks this year.

## The economy's shape

| Area | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Services (most of GDP)** | Real estate is the single largest industry (about 13% of GDP in late 2025). Finance, health, education, public administration and retail make up most of the rest. |
| **Natural resources** | Oil and gas (Alberta, Saskatchewan, Newfoundland), potash, uranium, gold, nickel, copper, critical minerals, forestry and agriculture. Mining and oil and gas were the main growth drivers in 2025. |
| **Manufacturing** | Autos and parts (Ontario), aerospace (Quebec), food processing, machinery, plastics, metals, wood and paper. Manufacturing shrank in 2025 as U.S. tariffs hit. |
| **Trade** | Highly export-dependent. About 48,000 enterprises exported goods in 2025. Over 97% are SMEs, and roughly 86% sell to the U.S. |
| **Population growth** | Driven by immigration (points-based Express Entry, students, temporary workers). This creates a constant flow of newcomers who must learn Canadian systems. |
| **Growth** | Real GDP grew 1.7% in 2025, the slowest since 2020, mainly because U.S.-bound exports weakened. |

## Canada vs the U.S. vs India vs the rest of the world

| Dimension | Canada | United States | India | Typical EU / rest of world | What it means for a startup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Who makes the rules** | 10 provinces + 3 territories hold real power over employment, sales tax, privacy, licensing and liquor. Internal trade barriers persist (only partly fixed by the 2025 *One Canadian Economy Act*). | Strong federal commerce rules; states vary on tax and employment. | Highly centralized; GST unified in 2017. | EU: single-market rules, national variation. | Canadian products must handle provinces from day one. One-size national tools often fail. |
| **Language** | Officially bilingual. Quebec's Charter (amended by **Bill 96**) makes French mandatory for commerce, with fines. It applies to firms with 25+ employees since June 2025. | No legal language mandate for business. | Many languages, mostly a market preference rather than a fined legal duty. | EU: national languages, rarely enforced on SMEs like Quebec. | A compliance product with a deadline and penalties that exists nowhere else. |
| **Sales tax** | Four regimes: GST (5%) everywhere; HST in Ontario and Atlantic Canada; separate PST in BC, Saskatchewan and Manitoba; QST in Quebec. Rates and registration rules differ. | No federal VAT; 45 states with sales tax and "economic nexus" thresholds. | Single national GST. | Single VAT per country (EU OSS for cross-border). | Small online sellers need help knowing where to register. Big tools (Avalara, Stripe Tax) handle collection, not "do I need to register?" |
| **Trade dependency** | Very high, and concentrated on the U.S. | Large, diversified, mostly domestic. | Moderate; exports diversified. | EU trades mostly within the bloc. | U.S. tariff swings hit Canadian SMEs immediately, so tariff tools are urgent. |
| **Banking** | Big Five/Six banks hold most deposits and mortgages. Interac is the dominant payment rail. Open banking framework still rolling out. | About 4,000 banks, many fintechs. | Public-sector banks + UPI fintech boom. | Mixed; PSD2 open banking. | Fintech consumer lanes are crowded and capital-heavy. Avoid them as a solo builder. |
| **Mortgages** | 5-year terms on 25-year loans: almost every homeowner **renews every few years** at whatever the rate is then. Prepayment penalties. | 30-year fixed; refinancing optional. | Mostly floating-rate home loans. | Varies; long fixed common in Western Europe. | Canada has a predictable, dated, high-stress decision for about 1 million households a year. |
| **Healthcare** | Single-payer, run provincially. | Private insurance + Medicare/Medicaid billing complexity. | Mixed public/private, out-of-pocket heavy. | Universal, mostly national. | No U.S.-style billing gap. Low fit. |
| **Agriculture** | **Supply management**: quotas and very high import tariffs on dairy, poultry and eggs. | Subsidies, no quotas. | Minimum support prices. | CAP subsidies. | A political flashpoint (U.S. now targets dairy with Section 338), not an SMB software gap. |
| **Alcohol and cannabis** | Provincial retail monopolies (LCBO, SAQ, BCLDB) and provincial cannabis retail rules. | Three-tier alcohol system by state. | State excise rules. | Mostly private retail. | Selling into each province is a separate listing process. Niche but real. |
| **Innovation support** | Generous **SR&ED** refundable tax credits and grants (IRAP, CanExport). | R&D credit, less generous for small firms. | Startup India schemes. | Horizon grants, national credits. | SMEs pay consultants 15–30% contingency to claim; a pre-check tool is a gap. |
| **Business culture and channels** | Trusts email, LinkedIn, industry associations (CFIB, CME), accountants and government portals. Pays for compliance. | Similar, larger, more competitive. | WhatsApp/Telegram, price-sensitive, mobile-first. | Association and accountant-led. | Sell through associations, accountants and brokers, not Telegram groups. Charge in CAD through Stripe (Razorpay does not operate in Canada). |

## What is happening right now (2026)

- **CUSMA was not renewed on July 1, 2026, but it is still in force.** The U.S. declined to extend it. The agreement continues (through 2036 unless a party withdraws on six months' notice), and annual reviews now apply. Uncertainty is high.
- **New U.S. tariffs override CUSMA.** On July 20, 2026 the U.S. signed three **Section 338** proclamations adding 50% duties on listed Canadian goods (alcohol, dairy and a broader list of roughly 569 tariff lines, about $20B of trade). Advisories report they took effect around August 22, 2026, and that CUSMA qualification gives **no relief**.
- **Section 232 sectoral tariffs** on steel, aluminum, copper, lumber, furniture and autos continue regardless of CUSMA (autos get partial relief).
- **The older IEEPA "fentanyl" tariff (35% on non-CUSMA goods) was struck down** by the U.S. Supreme Court on February 20, 2026. A temporary 10% Section 122 surcharge followed. Its status after its 150-day limit is unconfirmed (verify).
- **Small business sentiment:** CFIB surveys report widespread tariff harm. Nearly half of firms trading with the U.S. shifted toward non-U.S. suppliers or customers in the past year. The number of SMEs exporting to the U.S. fell 1.4% in 2025, while SMEs exporting elsewhere rose 6.6%.
- **Quebec French enforcement is rising:** a record 11,125 OQLF complaints in the year to March 2026, and an undercover-shopper study of about 7,800 businesses running through November 2026.
- **Mortgage renewals:** about 1.15 million mortgages renew in 2026 and 940,000 in 2027 (CMHC). Typical five-year fixed borrowers see payments rise 15–20%, and some see increases above 40%.
- **Internal trade is opening, slowly:** federal free-trade rules took effect January 1, 2026, and a mutual-recognition deal for goods was signed in November 2025. Food, alcohol and labour mobility remain largely unresolved.

## The one-line takeaway

> Canada rewards **narrow, rule-heavy, deadline-driven compliance tools** for small businesses. The rules are fragmented (provinces, French, four sales-tax regimes) and volatile (U.S. tariffs), incumbents serve large companies, and Canadian SMEs already pay professionals $150–400/hour to answer the same questions repeatedly.
