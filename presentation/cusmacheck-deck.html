2026: why now

The tariff rules changed four times this year

Feb 20U.S. Supreme Court strikes down the IEEPA tariffs (including the 35% on non-CUSMA Canadian goods). A temporary 10% Section 122 surcharge follows.
Jul 1The U.S. declines to renew CUSMA at the joint review. The agreement stays in force; annual reviews begin.
Jul 20 → ~Aug 22Section 338 proclamations add 50% duties that CUSMA does not remove on ~569 lines: alcohol, dairy and more (~$20B).
OngoingSection 232 tariffs: steel, aluminum, copper 50%; lumber 10%; furniture and cabinets 25%; autos 25%. CUSMA does not remove these either.

Sources: law-firm and logistics advisories (White & Case, Holland & Knight, C.H. Robinson, McMillan, Blakes). See docs/sources.md. Dates vary across sources; verify before external use.

The problem

"Does my product qualify, and what will I actually pay?" Nobody answers this cheaply.

Self-certification

Exporters certify CUSMA origin themselves. No form is required, so many don't know whether they qualify, and they either overpay duty or pay a broker for every new SKU.

Stacked tariffs

In 2026, qualifying no longer means duty-free. Section 232 and Section 338 apply on top, and the lists keep changing.

Expensive help

Brokers bill $150–400/hr. The government tool only looks up rates. Export software assumes you already know you qualify.

The solution

CUSMAcheck: structured input, rules engine, a verdict in dollars

1 · Enter

HS code, selling price, bill of materials (each input's HS code, cost and country of origin), shipment value.

→

2 · Rules engine

CUSMA criteria A/B/C/D, tariff-shift test with 10% de minimis, RVC by both methods. No AI: the treaty rules are fixed.

→

3 · Verdict + stack

Qualifies or not, with the rule cited. Each U.S. tariff layer, whether CUSMA removes it, and total duty in dollars. Logged for audit.

v1.1 adds AI only where it helps: plain-English explanations and Certificate of Origin drafting.

Format

A web app, not a Chrome extension

Extensions win when…

…the pain sits inside a page the user already has open (tender listings, Amazon pages, the GST portal).

Here the pain is…

…document-in, verdict-out. There is no host page to inject into, so it is a calculator over structured data with saved history.

What carries over

Node.js on Render, Supabase, Claude via the existing proxy. New: Stripe for CAD billing, since Razorpay does not operate in Canada.

Competition & risk

Clear white space, with risks we design around

Alternatives

Canada Tariff Finder (rates only) · brokers ($150–400/hr) · export-docs software (forms only) · CUSMA workshops (~700 firms) · enterprise trade suites (priced for large firms).

Accuracy risk

Advisory disclaimer, audit log, broker-verified rule data, E&O insurance, expert routing for autos and apparel.

Volatility risk

"Rates as of" on every result; scheduled refresh. Tariff Alerts turn volatility into a reason to subscribe.

Treaty risk

If CUSMA ends, the tariff-stack calculator still works; reposition as a U.S. duty calculator for Canadian exporters.
